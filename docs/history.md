# PyDevices Organization History

*Written 2026-09-06 and brought up to date through 2026-10-22 from GitHub's repository records, releases and merged pull requests, and the repositories' own histories; every creation date in the chronology was re-checked against GitHub on the day of each update. Update the chronology when a repository is created, transferred, renamed, archived or published.*

## Concise history

PyDevices began before the organization itself. The oldest project lineage starts on 2023-11-10 as Brad Barnett’s `mpdisplay` work: board configurations, display drivers, graphics, input events, and examples accumulated in one repository. CircuitPython support arrived in April 2024, followed by package extraction and broader desktop support.

On 2024-09-02 the code was rebranded “PyDevices”; GitHub records the organization’s creation the following day, 2024-09-03. On 2024-11-19 the original repository moved from `bdbarnett/mpdisplay` to `PyDevices/pydisplay`. Three supporting repositories followed: the imported LVGL MicroPython lineage, a `micropython-lib` fork, and `cmods`.

The organization assumed its current shape during June–August 2026. The large `pydisplay` repository was deliberately decomposed into display, LVGL, graphics, widget, palette, hardware, tooling, and examples repositories. An organization portal and shared publishing machinery were established in July; on 2026-08-11 the old flagship was refocused and renamed `pydevices-examples`, while the extracted hardware repository became the new `pydevices` flagship.

Late August and early September broadened the project substantially: Android and PyScript application paths, a browser/mobile Workbench, the CircuitPython-compatible audio stack, VST3 hosting, a versioned MicroPython overlay, native USB host/device support, and MIPI-CSI cameras. The audio component split shipped on 2026-09-03; `usbif` and `cameraif` were published on 2026-09-04.

September was a month of consolidation around the audio and timing layers. `mpvst` shipped its first installable release on 2026-09-11 and took that name the same day. On 2026-09-21 the audio core was renamed `audiodsp`, and the name it left, `audioif`, went to the audio hardware layer that sits under the audio pump, published that day. On 2026-09-23 `cmods` was archived: `micropython-pydevices`, created on 2026-08-29, had taken over the presets, variants and patch series that firmware builds needed. `pydevices` added Bluetooth Low Energy support on 2026-09-25 and, on 2026-09-26 in 0.6.0, the redesigned `multimer` timing layer, one timer and dispatcher per host across MicroPython, CircuitPython and CPython. `mpftp` learned to reach boards over Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on 2026-09-24 and 2026-09-25, and examples for casting a board's display and sound to a computer or a Roku TV followed on 2026-09-28.

October moved the native code and the packaging. On 2026-10-05 `micropython-pydevices` gained one build command, `build_mp.py`, that builds firmware by module name, and on 2026-10-08 it began building CircuitPython-compatible firmware as well as MicroPython. The media modules `jpegio`, `h264enc`, `tsmux`, `ppa` and `pngio` landed there between 2026-10-06 and 2026-10-07, along with HLS casting in `castif`. In `audiocomponents`, the rebuilt effects shipped in v0.4.0 on 2026-10-15, and v0.4.1 on 2026-10-22 added SoundFont instruments, drum kits that answer pad keys 36 to 51, and a MIDI dispatcher. On 2026-10-19 the four interface repositories were folded in and archived: the C modules of `usbif`, `audioif`, `cameraif` and `displayif` moved into `micropython-pydevices` under `modules/`, and the Python parts of `usbif` moved into `pydevices` as the `usb-*` packages. The same day `pydevices` 0.7.6 was released as a final, with MIDI ports on `adafruit_midi`, Bluetooth MIDI and HID on `adafruit_midi` and `adafruit_hid`, USB on `usb.core`, the camera under `espcamera`, and I2S capture. On 2026-10-20 `palettes` and `pdwidgets` moved into `pydevices` and were archived, and a package-library builder (`tools/libbuild.py`) was merged there; the `lib` repository began serving its output as a `mip` index on 2026-10-21, and board configurations and drivers became installable packages of that library in the same days. On 2026-10-22 `micropython-pydevices` gained dev-container configurations for building firmware in a codespace, and `pydevices` 0.7.7, the first final to attach desktop and WebAssembly interpreters, was released.

The 2014 history in `mip`, the 2018 history in `lvgl-micropython`, and the 2024 history in `workbench` are inherited upstream histories—not evidence that PyDevices itself existed at those dates.
